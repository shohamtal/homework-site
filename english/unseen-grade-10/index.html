Cities That Grow Food on Rooftops

Look up the next time you walk through a big city, and you might see something unexpected: a farm. Across the world, flat roofs that were once empty are being gardens full of vegetables, herbs and even fruit trees. This is called urban farming, and it is growing fast. In New York, one rooftop farm produces about twenty thousand kilos of vegetables every year. In Singapore, where land is and expensive, the government buildings to grow food on their roofs. Even in Tel Aviv, a shopping centre has a farm on its roof that sells fresh lettuce to nearby restaurants. Supporters say that rooftop farms have several . First, the food does not have to travel hundreds of kilometres by truck, so it reaches people fresher and causes less . Second, plants on a roof the sun's heat, which keeps the building cooler in summer and lowers electricity bills. In addition, the gardens give birds and bees a place to live in the middle of and glass. Some farms also run workshops where children learn where their food actually comes from. However, growing food in the sky is not simple. Not every roof is strong enough to carry the of wet soil, and checking this can be costly. Water must be pumped up, and strong winds can damage young plants. Many rooftop farms use hydroponics, a method in which plants grow in water with instead of soil. This makes them lighter and saves up to ninety percent of the water that a normal field needs. Critics point out that rooftops can never traditional farms, because there is simply not enough space. A whole city could not feed itself from its roofs. Still, the people behind these projects believe that this is not the point. For them, every roof garden is a small step toward greener, healthier cities, and a that nature can grow almost anywhere.
